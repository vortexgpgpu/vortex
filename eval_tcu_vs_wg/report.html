WMMA vs WGMMA on rtlsim

sgemm_tcu (WMMA) vs sgemm_tcu_wg (WGMMA) vs sgemm_tcu_wg_dxa (WGMMA+DXA) vs +FEDP2K — 128×128×128, fp16→fp32, NW=16, NT ∈ {4, 8, 16}, ISSUE_WIDTH ∈ {1, 2, 4}, --perf=1. Re-measured 2026-09-17 on the improved architecture (grouped DXA scatter drain fa1765c85 + register-resident kernel pipeline bd1420351), now including the NT=32 extension: 27/27 improved runs PASSED on top of the original 33/33 (NT=4+FEDP2K structurally unsupported; NT=32 baselines measured in the same batch)

Verdict (improved architecture): WGMMA+DXA now beats WMMA at every high-occupancy point. Two fixes landed since the original evaluation — a grouped BlockMajor scatter drain in the DXA engine (one masked LMEM write per n-group instead of per element) and a register-resident kernel schedule — and the sweep inverts the original verdict: WGMMA+DXA wins 5 of 12 points outright and every point with NT≥16 and IW≥2. The new NT=32 extension — the architecture’s canonical warp width — is its best showing: 63,412 cycles at NT=32/IW=4, 1.58× faster than WMMA (99,927; 66.1 vs 42.0 FLOP/cycle), with WMMA now flat-to-inverse in both NT and IW (occupancy-bound at 53K instructions). At NT=16/IW=4 the margin is 1.49× (74,947 vs 111,373), and DXA improves 1.15–2.3× over its pre-fix self at every point measured in both sweeps. The original verdict ("WMMA wins all 27 points, DXA closes to 1.08×") described the pre-fix architecture; the instruction-efficiency story it identified (6–15× fewer retired instructions) was real and is what the drain fix finally converts into cycles. Remaining losses are the low-occupancy corner (NT≤8, IW≤2), where single-warp-group CTAs forfeit data reuse — the known CTA-width/ISSUE_WIDTH coupling, not the DMA path.

Why: instruction overhead is the whole story

  • The tensor datapath is never the limit. TCU stall stays ≤8% in every run of every variant. All separation between the three kernels comes from how operands reach the TCU.
  • Plain WGMMA is issue-starved: IW 1→2 buys it 2.8–2.9×; its instruction count falls with IW (the spin-poll signature). WGMMA+DXA’s instruction count is nearly IW-invariant (296K→272K at NT=4) — the polling is mostly gone.
  • DXA turns stores into DMA. Plain WGMMA issues 148K–673K stores (LMEM staging); WGMMA+DXA issues exactly 17,408 in every configuration — only the C-tile results. Loads drop 5–10× likewise, and load latency holds at 7–21 cycles.
  • WMMA’s ceiling is occupancy, WGMMA+DXA’s is DMA latency. WMMA at NT=16 idles 66–71% because 53K instructions can’t fill 16 warps; WGMMA+DXA idles 83–94% waiting on transfers. Different bottlenecks — and the improved architecture resolves the DMA one: with the grouped drain the transfer wait shrinks enough that WGMMA+DXA pulls decisively ahead at NT=16/IW=4 (74,947 vs 111,373).
  • Issue width matters most for WGMMA+DXA: IW 1→4 gives it 2.6–2.8× at every NT (vs 1.2–1.8× for WMMA): with few instructions and long dependency chains, draining the ready pool fast is everything.

Warp-width scaling

FEDP2K variant: broken as found, fixed, then measured

Adding -DVX_CFG_TCU_FEDP2K (doubles the warp-group dense FEDP width) to the DXA series initially produced no valid data. Root-causing it surfaced two distinct upstream defects, both now fixed on this branch:

  • DXA scatter unaware of FEDP2K (commit 7d6977bb6): the BlockMajor B-tile destination math in VX_dxa_smem_wr (and the simx model) derived the block K-depth from lg_tcN via the square-block identity tcK = tcN, while vx_tensor.h::b_blockmajor_idx doubles it under FEDP2K — every DXA-staged element past the first tcK of a block landed in the wrong position (~16,383/16,384 outputs wrong, identically on simx and rtlsim). Fixed by folding an LG_FEDP_RATIO constant into the five BlockMajor destination terms on both sides.
  • bbuf could not hold a doubled block at NT=16 (commit 11e1afc8d): at NT=16/fp16 a FEDP2K B block is 32 words against 16 LMEM banks — two bank-rows — which the dense fetch path rejected at elaboration (this also broke plain sgemm_tcu_wg+FEDP2K on RTL, DXA or not). Fixed by extending the dense path with the sparse path’s two-slot machinery plus a b_off_wg wrap guard in VX_tcu_core.

Post-fix validation (rtlsim): wg and wg_dxa with FEDP2K pass at NT=8/16/32; non-FEDP2K dense and sparse configurations re-verified unchanged. NT=4+FEDP2K remains structurally unsupported and still fails loudly at elaboration — correct behavior, and why the FEDP2K series has no NT=4 bars.

Measured result: FEDP2K is performance-neutral here. It trims another ~10–12% off the instruction count (halved K-steps → fewer, wider MMA uops) and gains 0–5% cycles over WGMMA+DXA (best at narrow issue: −4.8% at NT=16/IW=1; +0.04% at NT=16/IW=4, i.e. 120,770 vs 120,723 cycles). The series is DMA-wait bound (83–93% scheduler idle), so widening the FEDP doesn’t move the bottleneck at this matrix size — its value would surface in issue-bound regimes or larger tiles.

Improved-architecture update (2026-09-17): the grouped scatter drain applies to FEDP2K wherever its doubled n-group still fits one LMEM word. At NT=8 it fits, and FEDP2K now edges out even improved base DXA (187,995 vs 192,680 cycles at IW=4 — itself faster than WMMA). At NT=16 the doubled group spans two LMEM words, so the engine takes the guarded element-wise fallback and FEDP2K sees only the kernel-side gains (116,617 vs 120,770 pre-fix at IW=4). Extending the grouped drain to a two-word emission is the identified follow-up that brings NT=16+FEDP2K in line.

NT=32 confirmation: at the canonical warp width the FEDP2K block (128 B) exactly matches the LMEM DMA word (32 lanes × 4 B), the grouped drain applies again, and the measured penalty is gone: 98,370 / 73,427 / 63,501 cycles at IW=1/2/4 vs base DXA’s 99,371 / 73,986 / 63,412 — within 1% everywhere, ahead at IW≤2. This pins the NT=16 regression on layout/port misalignment, not on the FEDP2K datapath. Caveat: these NT=32 comparisons are rtlsim-only — the simx model writes fixed 64-byte blocks and does not scale its DXA write granularity with the LMEM word, so it shows a phantom FEDP2K penalty at NT=32; a small model fix (match DXA_LMEM_WORD_SIZE) is the identified follow-up.

Attacking the DXA-wait bottleneck: double-buffering

The WGMMA+DXA series is DMA-wait bound (83–94% scheduler idle), so the obvious lever is a software pipeline: double-buffer the smem tiles and prefetch tile i+1 via DXA while computing tile i. Implemented behind DXA_DOUBLE_BUFFER in sgemm_tcu_wg_dxa (commit 780e3fdf3) — and measured, it loses decisively:

Why overlap backfires on this architecture: (1) the continuously-busy DXA stream owns the fixed-priority LMEM DMA port and DRAM channels that compute-side loads share — load latency inflates 16–22×; (2) the shared warp-group B buffer caches a single bank-row keyed by the B descriptor, and alternating buffers defeats that reuse every iteration; (3) the two-buffer control flow spills to stack, tripling dynamic loads into the already-contended dcache. Making prefetch overlap pay would need overlap-aware arbitration (bounded DXA priority), a multi-descriptor bbuf, and DXA rate limiting — follow-up architecture work, not a kernel tweak.

Resolved (2026-09-17): the follow-up investigation overturned this diagnosis in a useful way. The double-buffer loss was not an overlap problem but a register-residency one: the runtime-indexed buffer/barrier arrays spilled to the per-lane stacks, which live in global memory at an 8 KB stride, so one spilled word cost sixteen uncoalesced same-bank DRAM misses. A register-resident rewrite (XOR-toggled pointers and barrier ids, raw barrier intrinsics, zero hot-loop stack traffic; commit bd1420351) turned the 8.3× regression into a small win, and the DXA-side drain fix (commit fa1765c85) removed the engine bottleneck that made overlap moot. On the improved architecture the single-buffer schedule reaches 74,947 cycles at NT=16/IW=4 and double-buffering is safe and within noise of it (75,052) — its payoff now waits on the next wall, the tensor operand-buffer fetch path. Full accounts: the critical-path report, the double-buffering proposal and the rtlsim drain proposal.

The investigation also surfaced and fixed two real defects (both committed): a simx issue-interlock bug — the FU lock released at issue of the unlock uop, letting the operand collectors reorder another CTA’s WGMMA uop ahead of an in-flight instruction’s final uop into the TCU FIFO, where the CTA fence deadlocked the core (6bd7f4536); and an unqualified DXA completion counter in VX_mem_unit that would miscount under any future LMEM DMA back-pressure (02d54d393).

Method

Branch tcu-op (rebased on upstream master 1cd71c402), rtlsim driver, one core. Per configuration: CONFIGS="-DVX_CFG_NUM_THREADS=<NT> -DVX_CFG_NUM_WARPS=16 -DVX_CFG_ISSUE_WIDTH=<IW> -DVX_CFG_EXT_TCU_ENABLE -DVX_CFG_TCU_WGMMA_ENABLE [-DVX_CFG_EXT_DXA_ENABLE] -DITYPE=fp16 -DOTYPE=fp32 -DWGMMA_NRC=16", app rebuilt per config, then ./ci/blackbox.sh --driver=rtlsim --app=<app> --args="-m 128 -n 128 -k 128" --perf=1. TCU type: TFR (synthesizable FEDP, config default). All 27 runs PASSED against a host reference. Logs, results.json, and plot sources: ~/dev/vortex_spg/eval_tcu_vs_wg/. Improved-architecture re-measurement (2026-09-17): same commands and configurations on the branch with commits fa1765c85 (grouped BlockMajor scatter drain, DXA engine) and bd1420351 (register-resident kernel schedule; single-buffer default measured here); WMMA and plain-WGMMA rows are unchanged by construction (neither touches those paths). 15 runs, all PASSED; logs in eval_tcu_vs_wg/improved/. NT=32 extension (12 further runs: all four kernels at IW∈{1,2,4}, NW=16, i.e. 512 threads/core): all PASSED; the NT=32 WMMA and plain-WGMMA baselines come from this batch since the original sweep stopped at NT=16. The improved series was additionally validated by a 162-case WGMMA/DXA CI matrix (simx+rtlsim, XLEN 32 and 64, all passing). Caveats: single core; WGMMA_NRC fixed at 16, not tuned per config; DXA hardware present only in the wg_dxa builds (it is required by that kernel and unused by the others); NT=4/8 are below the 32-lane warp size upstream CI uses for warp-group tests; SimX is known to over-serialize the DXA-fed pipeline by ~6% (known_issue in dxa.yaml) — irrelevant here since all runs are rtlsim.
