# No placement wins everywhere

**Three axes, one GEMM harness, and no placement that owns more than part of the grid.**

This document replaces the single-winner framing the earlier figures carried. The question
is not which unit is fastest — it is *which unit is fastest for which work*, and the answer
changes along every axis measured here. The third axis, concurrency, turned out to have a
hard wall rather than an optimum, and that wall is the most useful thing in the document.

The two placement diagrams are unchanged from `260807_dtcu_figures.html`; the experiments
and the livelock figure are new.

## 2026-08-27 control-path completion

The mode-3/mode-5 controls now complete the full 8-shape × 7-epilogue grid: **112/112
`ok`, 112/112 `errors=0`** in
[`exp1_m3_m5_final_20260827.csv`](../result/260827_data/exp1_m3_m5_final_20260827.csv).

**Two placements of the same descriptor engine.** `DTCU_socket` puts one engine in each
socket and writes *D* into that socket's L1; `DTCU_cluster` puts one in the cluster and
writes to L2. With `SOCKET_SIZE = 1` on a four-core cluster that is four engines against
one, so the pair separates *where D lands* from *how many engines the placement buys*.

**Modes 1 and 5 keep the work in the cores** and differ in how much reuse the geometry
gives them: mode 1 loads every fragment through the LSU, mode 5 stages its A rows for the

and 8 hand the GEMM to an engine**; the core only submits a descriptor and polls.

## The axes

**Axis 1 — shape, through arithmetic intensity.** For a GEMM,

```
AI = M·N·K / (M·K + N·K + M·N)   →   ~K   as M and N grow
```

so **K decides whether a shape is memory-bound or compute-bound**. Attention's `QK⊤` has
`M = N = seqlen` against `K = head_dim`, and head_dim does not grow when the sequence does —
that family stays memory-bound at every size. A cube where all three grow together becomes
compute-bound. A cubic ladder is therefore a *machine-scaling probe, not a workload*, and is
labelled as one here.

**Axis 2 — the epilogue, and it has four tiers.** (Axis 3, concurrency, has its own
section after the results.)

| tier | apps | who can fuse it |
|---|---|---|
| **none** | 1 | — |
| **elementwise** | 2 ReLU, 3 GELU | the in-core modes fold it into the accumulator in registers; the DTCU has no epilogue hardware and pays a **second full launch** over *D* |
| **elementwise + an operand** | 4 residual `D += R`, 5 per-channel scale `D *= s[col]` | same fold, but a **second array must be streamed in** alongside *C*. 4 reads a full *M × N* matrix, 5 reads an *N*-vector — the same tier with two very different traffic costs |
| **reduction** | 6 row-wise softmax, 9 per-channel bias | **nobody**. A tile holds part of a row and part of a column, so neither statistic exists until every tile is written. Every mode pays an extra pass, and the DTCU modes pay a **third** |

Apps 4 and 5 need an operand `kernel_arg_t` has no pointer for, and the struct is fixed at
64 B. `MOTI_AUX_ELEM_OFFSET(M, N)` in `common.h` is the route taken instead: the host appends
the array behind *C* in the same buffer and the kernel derives the address, so a new operand
costs a `#define` and no field.

The two reduction apps differ in *direction*, and that is deliberate. A row lives inside one
socket, because the engines slice *M* four ways — so app 6 never tests where *D* landed. A
column touches every row, so under app 9 the socket engines read across into each other's L1
while the cluster engine's *D* is already in L2 where all four cores reach it alike. App 9 is
the bias gradient's access pattern, and it is the one built to interrogate the placement.

**The epilogue is chosen by the preprocessor, one per binary.** `MOTI_APP` selects it at
compile time, so a binary contains exactly one and an app the run does not use cannot
relocate a mode's code in the icache — carrying two unused epilogue kernels moved mode 4 by
+44.6 %. Each column below is its own build.

**Apps 7 and 8 are not built.** They are the quantised pair, and they need a GEMM that
accumulates `int32` while storing `fp32`. Turning on `VX_CFG_TCU_INT8_ENABLE` is necessary
and not sufficient: this harness carries a single `OTYPE` for both the accumulator and the
output, so the two cannot differ until that is split.

**Three different modes win, and the epilogue moves the boundary as much as the shape
does.** The middle cube alone makes the point: at 512 × 256 × 128 the winner is mode 7 with
no epilogue, mode 5 with ReLU, and mode 1 with GELU or a residual — three placements over
one shape, chosen by nothing but what happens to *D* on the way out.

- **The engine owns the memory-bound end.** At the small cube it takes every app. At
  256 × 256 × 64 — attention-shaped, so memory-bound however large it gets — it takes app 1
  by 2.14× over the in-core TCU, and at 1024 × 1024 × 64 by 2.08×.
- **An elementwise epilogue narrows or reverses that.** At 256 × 256 × 64 ReLU costs mode 5
  *nothing* (97,515 → 97,470) and costs the engine **+28.7 %**, which hands it the cell. The
  engine pays in memory traffic where the in-core modes pay in arithmetic.
- **An epilogue with a second operand is worse for the engine still.** App 5 costs mode 1
  **+190 cycles** at 128 × 64 × 32 against mode 7's +4,444; at the large cube app 4 goes to
  mode 1 outright.
- **The compute-bound end belongs to the cores.** At 768 × 384 × 192 mode 5 beats the engine
  by 1.38× on app 1, and with GELU attached plain mode 1 takes it back.
- **A reduction epilogue levels almost everything.** Nobody can fuse it, so the extra pass

### Axis 3 — concurrency, and the arbiter that forbids it

Everything above measures the engine and the cores taking turns. They have to: the runtime
polls a launch to retirement (`queue.cpp:431`), so two launches never overlap, and modes 7
and 8 issue one descriptor covering a whole row band and then spin on its single `done`.
Every core is idle for the entire GEMM, and the epilogue can only run afterwards.

**Modes 14 and 15** cut the band into `MOTI_PIPE_TILES` descriptors so it has that many
completion points, and run the epilogue for slice *t−1* while the engine produces slice *t*.
`dtensor_check()` is a plain AMO rather than an engine instruction, so a core that never
issued a descriptor can wait on one — no extra protocol is needed. The two are deliberately
asymmetric, and the asymmetry is the experiment: mode 14 has four engines and a core can
only reach its own, so every core is a producer and nobody is spare; mode 15 has one engine,
so core 0 alone feeds it and the rest of the machine is free to consume.

#### The consumer sweep, and the wall in it

How much of each core should consume? Two layouts were built. **Row-striped** gives every
warp its own rows; **core-striped** keeps a core on one row at a time and splits that row's
columns across its threads. They differ only in indexing, and they are not close:

| consumer warps<br>per core | row-striped<br>256 × 256 × 64 | row-striped<br>768 × 384 × 192 | core-striped<br>*width of one row* |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| 1 | 355,424 | **1,520,310** | — |
| 2 | **322,041** | ✗ | — |
| 4 | 324,683 | ✗ | — |
| 8 | ✗ | ✗ | — |
| 16 | ✗ | ✗ | **fastest at every shape** |

**`✗` is not a timeout. It is a livelock, and it is the result.** Those runs were still
burning 199 % CPU after four hours, reproducibly, for a GEMM whose healthy configurations
finish in under a minute. The cause is one line:

```
sim/simx/cluster.cpp:153   MemArbiter::Create(sname, ArbiterType::Priority, ...)
      sockets → row 0          DXA → row 1
      DTCU_cluster → row 2     DTCU_socket → row 3
```

**The L2 arbiter is strict priority and the cores sit above both engines.** Once the
consumers keep row 0 non-empty, the cluster engine on row 2 never gets a slot; it stops
making progress, `done` never sets, and the consumers keep polling — which keeps row 0 busy.
*The consumers starve the producer they are waiting on.* Isolated three ways: with an empty
consumer body the same configuration finishes, with GELU it hangs, and removing the
descriptor read-back changes nothing.

What sets the threshold is **cache lines in flight per core**, not warps. Row-striping puts
16 rows' worth in flight at once; core-striping puts one row's. That is why the core-striped
layout takes all 16 warps at every shape and is also simply faster — 1,351,467 cycles
against 2,511,251 at 768 × 384 × 192 with GELU.

#### Replaying the tuning history at the largest cell

The progression above was measured at 128 × 64 × 32, which is the worst shape for this mode
— four engine tiles in the whole GEMM, so nothing to overlap and fixed cost dominates. Run
again at 1024 × 1024 × 64 with app 5, two of its conclusions do not survive:

| configuration | cycles | vs default |
|---|--:|--:|
| core-striped, width 16, core 0 idle, **read-back off** | **2,769,522** | −0.3 % |
| core-striped, width 16, core 0 idle *(the default)* | 2,776,821 | — |
| row-striped, width 1, core 0 idle | 2,997,109 | +7.9 % |
| core-striped, width 16, **core 0 consumes** | 3,019,139 | +8.7 % |
| core-striped, **width 1**, core 0 idle | 3,189,018 | +14.8 % |
| row-striped, width 16, core 0 consumes | ✗ | — |

- **The descriptor read-back is free here.** At the small cell it cost 120,000 cycles, half
  of that run. Here it costs 7,299 — the same fixed amount over four descriptors, against a
  much larger denominator. It was never the regression it looked like.
- **Row-striping is not always fatal.** At width 1 it finishes, and beats *core*-striping at
  width 1. The livelock boundary is the ratio of slice rows to consumers, not the shape.

What does hold at both cells: adding the producing core to the consumer pool loses, and
narrowing the width loses.

#### So why is there a producer core at all?

There is not, really. Core 0 fills four descriptors and issues four engine instructions —
a few hundred cycles — and then returns. It does **not** watch for completion; each consumer
core polls the descriptor it needs, because `dtensor_check()` is an ordinary AMO and needs
no intermediary.

It is left idle because using it is worse: 3 consumer cores beat 4 by 8.7 %. The fourth
core's traffic takes more from the engine than its arithmetic gives back. **The cluster
engine is bandwidth-limited enough that the optimal number of consumer cores on this machine
is three out of four** — the machine cannot use itself.

### The cluster engine, and two configuration errors found by asking about it### The cluster engine, and two configuration errors found by asking about it

`DTCU_cluster` is one engine with a 64 × 32 tile writing *D* to L2; `DTCU_socket` is four
engines with a 32 × 16 tile writing to the producing socket's L1. The pair is supposed to
isolate **placement**. Twice it was not doing that.

**First, the accumulator SRAM width was one global `#define` at 2** — the same for both,
while the cluster tile has four times the output area. `estimate_execute_cycles_()` takes a
`max()` of three stages and the accumulator won it outright: 2,049 cycles against the MAC
array's 1,024. The array could be widened without limit and nothing would move, which is
exactly what an earlier probe measured: doubling the PE count changed the cluster by
**0.00 %**. That null result was the clue.

**Second, and only visible once the first was fixed: the array itself was still half.** At
4 banks and 4 PEs the cluster sustained 31.8 MAC/cyc against the four socket engines' 63.3,
and the measurement showed that and nothing else —

| 8 / 7, app 1 | 128×64×32 | 512×256×128 | 256×256×64 | 768×384×192 | 1024×1024×64 |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| ratio | 2.64× | 2.03× | 2.05× | **2.00×** | 1.99× |

1.99–2.05× is the array ratio to three digits. **There was no placement signal in it at
all**, and reporting that the placement lost while its silicon was halved would have been a
statement about nothing.

**The cluster engine now gets the array of the four socket engines it stands in for** —
8 PEs and 8 accumulator banks against 4 × (2, 2) — so both sustain **63.26 MAC/cyc**:

| app 1 | half array | matched | mode 7 | 8 / 7 |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 128 × 64 × 32 | 30,980 | **21,780** | 11,728 | 1.86× |
| 512 × 256 × 128 | 614,986 | **378,490** | 303,303 | **1.25×** |
| 256 × 256 × 64 | 180,258 | **123,134** | 87,782 | 1.40× |
| 768 × 384 × 192 | 2,004,892 | **1,195,588** | 1,002,507 | **1.19×** |
| 1024 × 1024 × 64 | 3,024,854 | **2,164,674** | 1,523,464 | 1.42× |

Modes 0, 1, 5 and 7 are untouched — no other mode reads a cluster parameter — so the whole
improvement is isolated to the thing that was mis-configured.

**It still never wins.** Against the best of modes 1/5/7 it lands 1.02× to 1.86× behind.
What is left after the array is matched is the **port topology**, and that is where the
placement difference actually lives in this model.

#### Where the placement difference lives, and where it does not

Four things in the simulator depend on which engine it is: the descriptor queue depth, the
PE count, the accumulator bank count, and a debug string. Plus one in the plumbing --
`socket.cpp:188-190` binds the socket engine a dedicated `d_req_out` port into its socket's
dcache, and `dtcu_tma.h:117` routes its *D* stores down it. The cluster engine never gets
that call, so its *D* leaves on the same L2 port as its operands:

| | engines | ports each | total |
|---|--:|---|--:|
| 7 DTCU_socket | 4 | L2 + a dedicated *D* port | **8** |
| 8 DTCU_cluster | 1 | L2, carrying *D* too | **1** |

**So no epilogue can ever separate the two placements**, and the reduction column above is
not the encouraging sign it looks like. The proof is the *absolute* cost of the epilogue,
app N minus app 1, per mode:

| 128 × 64 × 32 | mode 1 | mode 5 | mode 7 | mode 8 |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| app 3 GELU | +4,791 | +17,974 | **+8,631** | **+8,631** |
| app 6 softmax | +229,883 | +230,873 | **+232,561** | **+232,561** |
| app 9 bias | +47,694 | +47,798 | **+48,329** | **+48,329** |

Identical to the cycle, and identical again at 256 × 256 × 64 (+1,276,598 each for softmax).
A standalone epilogue launch cannot see where *D* was produced. The 1.02× at
1024 × 1024 × 64 is not the placement closing a gap; it is a fixed 18.9 M-cycle pass
diluting a fixed 641 k-cycle one.

An earlier version of this document said app 9 "confirms the placement mechanism, +107 % for
mode 7 against +40 % for mode 8". Those are the same absolute number over different
denominators. **Withdrawn.**

### What is not here

- **Apps 7 and 8 are not built.** The quantised pair needs a GEMM accumulating `int32` and
  storing `fp32`; the harness has one `OTYPE` for both, so enabling
  `VX_CFG_TCU_INT8_ENABLE` is necessary and not sufficient.
- **The warp-count sweep has never been run.** `NUM_WARPS` at 8 / 16 / 32 would move the
  in-core modes and not the engine modes, so every in-core number here is at one occupancy.
- **Modes 2, 3 and 4 are measured but not shown.** They are controls — 2 isolates DXA without
  the geometry that makes it pay, 4 isolates the geometry without DXA, 3 is mode 5 without
  A-residency. They belong in the argument, not in a table of candidates.
- **`—` is a six-hour timeout, and 22 of the 140 cells hit it.** They cluster in mode 5 and
  in app 9, and the `†` markers in the tables say which winners were decided over an
  incomplete field.
- **Roughly a fifth of sweep points in this harness never terminate**, at shapes with no
  discernible pattern, and mode 1 is the only mode that has never done it. Every ratio above
  is drawn from the subset that ran. This is unexplained and tracked separately.
